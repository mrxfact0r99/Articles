The $25 Bounty Lesson 💰

About a year ago, as a beginner in bug bounty, I started applying my learning to real-world scenarios. I searched for bug bounty programs online using specific keywords. One day, I stumbled upon a WordPress website and tested a few endpoints:

https://target.nl/wp/wp-admin/install.php
https://target.nl/wp/wp-admin/upgrade.php

Vulnerability Description

When I accessed the install.php endpoint, I saw this message:
“Already Installed. You appear to have already installed WordPress. To reinstall, please clear your old database tables first.”

Similarly, on upgrade.php, it displayed:
“No Update Required. Your WordPress database is already up to date!”

These messages exposed critical information about the site’s WordPress installation status and version.

Impact

This information, while seemingly harmless, could be exploited by attackers or automated tools to:

  • Gather insights into the website’s infrastructure.
  • Identify vulnerabilities tied to the specific WordPress version, increasing the risk of exploitation.

Outcome

At the time, the company did not accept my report, labeling it as non-rewardable. I was disappointed but took it as a learning experience and moved on.

Fast forward one year—I received an email from the same company. They informed me that my report was in triage mode, and their developers were working to fix it. Days later, they patched the bug and rewarded me with a small bounty of $25!

Even though the amount wasn’t huge, the experience taught me a powerful lesson:
"Never lose hope. Trust in your work, and have faith in your efforts. Results will come, even if delayed."
